A STUDY IN ECCLESIASTES

LIVING LIFE BACKWARD

How Ecclesiastes teaches us to live in light of the end

DAVID GIBSON — TEN CHAPTERS
TEN WEEKS · SMALL GROUP GUIDE

WELCOME

Over the next ten weeks, we'll work through Ecclesiastes together, one chapter at a time, and talk honestly about what it means to live with the end in view.

Come as you are. Unpack your thoughts as you read.

THE BOOK

LIVING LIFE BACKWARD

DAVID GIBSON

Gibson walks through Ecclesiastes arguing that facing death honestly, not avoiding it, is what teaches us to receive life as a gift instead of chasing it as a prize.

Each week we'll sit with one chapter, read the Preacher's argument on its own terms, and ask what it means for how we spend our days.

FORMAT

HOW WE'LL USE THIS GUIDE

01
Chapter dividers

Each chapter opens with its passage and a key verse to read aloud together.

02
Five questions

Discussion questions follow each chapter, feel free to share as we unpack these lessons.

03
Track your progress

We will check off chapters as we go through the book. 

BEFORE CHAPTER ONE — THE PREFACE

DISCUSSION INTRODUCTION

“Only if you prepare to die can you really learn how to live.”

1

Gibson opens by writing, “I am ready to die.” What's your honest first reaction to that sentence?

2

He says he isn't afraid of his own death, but worries about losing people close to him. Do you feel that same gap?

3

“The end will put it all right.” How might trusting an ending change how you carry an unresolved mess today?

4

What do you think it means to “live life backward” working from your death into today's decisions?
